# DEC-0012 — The base-image repin: the pins stand, and a diagnosis becomes a hypothesis

Arrived as the sixth governed ferry shipment (`SHIP-20260928T135059`), re-shipped with
the enactment signature the fifth attempt lacked. Signed by the owner's merge (PA-002,
ENT-079).

## The custody line, and why this ruling had to arrive twice

```
custody        Good "ferry" signature — MacThulhu-16-4 (SHA256:AQ2dd3a7…),
               enrolled 2026-09-28T18:47:43Z, granted-by kscott
content        cargo 04d69d3064daaaad… equals the manifest's pinned digest, and is
               byte-identical to the copy held unconsumed since the fifth attempt
authorization  auth record signed by that same enrolled machine; its manifest_digest
               9404b79e7bd92876… equals the manifest that landed, binding duo txid
               f6b668d3… to these exact bytes
```

The fifth attempt shipped from a machine the receipt itself had ruled stale, and so
carried no auth record. Reception held it rather than consuming it, for a reason worth
keeping: the receipt had declared that **the Duo approval *is* this ruling's enactment
signature**, and a ruling whose enactment signature is absent from the shipment is not a
ruling that has been enacted. Custody and content both verified on that attempt. The
authorization third did not, and two thirds of a custody line is a finding, not a pass.

That is the ferry's own lifecycle working as designed. `RECEIVED` is not terminal, the
bytes arriving is not the shipment being consumable, and the month the ruling spent in
`proposed/` is the cost of the rule holding rather than evidence against it.

## Act 1 is withdrawn, on a premise the world disproved

`INSTRUCTION_base_image_repin_v0.1` §1 asserted that the registry had ceased serving the
pinned digest — a retention removal. Reception's verification showed a transient
availability window instead: the digest resolves, and the required checks have since run
green on the identical pins, repeatedly, without any pin being touched.

So Act 1 is **withdrawn, unexecuted, on a disproven premise** — not completed, not
superseded, and not quietly dropped. The distinction matters because the withdrawal is
the record of an error, and the error is worth naming precisely: a checkable world-fact
was written into an instruction without being checked. The floor records the
misdiagnosis as the Architect's.

**The agent's refusal to execute is affirmed as correct.** This is the half that is easy
to get backwards. The instruction was properly authorized — signed by an enrolled
machine, approved by the principal, digest-bound on arrival. Its custody chain was
flawless and its central factual claim was false, because *a custody chain proves who
ordered something and never that the order's claims are true* (B-1: artifacts, not
authority). An agent that executes an authorized instruction against failed evidence has
mistaken authorization for correctness. Refusing, and saying why, is the behaviour the
ferry was built to make possible.

## Act 2 is chartered, not built

Mirror-on-pin enters as `proposed` — STORY-0019 — carrying this incident as its evidence
base, with the failure mode stated more sharply than the instruction stated it: the
exposure is **any upstream availability event landing on a required check**, not
retention removal specifically. A transient recurs and announces nothing; a deletion at
least announces itself. Mirroring at pin time closes both, and closing only the
announced one would have left the case that actually happened wide open.

It is chartered rather than built because the ruled build queue is the ruled build queue:
it sits behind CTRL-0008 → STORY-0011 → STORY-0005, and is promoted immediately if the
transient recurs. A charter that jumped its own queue on the strength of the incident
that produced it would make every incident a queue-jumping instrument.

## The drafting law, third of the set

> **Diagnoses in instructions are hypotheses until tested** — either verified before
> drafting, or written as an Act 0 verification with the remedy conditioned on its result.

It is recorded in `INSTRUCTION_LAYER.md`, where the instruction layer's governance lives,
joining "cargo never asserts its own transport" and "gate facts verified or labeled
unverified".

**Recorded as prose, and not minted as a claim — deliberately.** A claim of this kind is
`check: human`, and CTRL-0012 now exists to evidence exactly that, so atomizing it is
available. The meter is not the obstacle: `unbound_claims` counts *ratified* claims, so a
proposed one moves nothing. Ratifying it is what would take the meter from 11 to 12
unless a rule bound it in the same act — and DEC-0011 is the record of that exact
arithmetic going wrong, with the eleven held unbound on purpose because SPEC-0117 ties
reaching zero to the owner's reviewing pass rather than to rules being written. So this
is a live choice about the floor's meter, taken by the floor, not slipped in under a
ruling that said *recorded*.

**Two of the three siblings are not in this corpus.** "Cargo never asserts its own
transport" and "gate facts verified or labeled unverified" appear nowhere in
`platform/corpus/`. They are drafting-plane law that has never crossed. This note is the
finding, not a fix: importing rules the floor did not ship in this shipment would be the
same overreach in the other direction.

Chartered by DEC-0012 and deliberately left `proposed`. The build slot is behind

**The evidence base is the incident that produced it**, which is also why the scope is
wider than the incident: the instruction reached for retention removal, and what actually
happened was a transient window. Pinning a digest makes a build reproducible and does not
make it *available* — availability stays a property of somebody else's registry, on the
path of a required check. Mirroring at pin time is what converts an upstream outage from
a merge-gate outage into a cache hit.

One criterion is cut, not a set. The schema requires at least one — a story with no
acceptance criterion is a wish — and the risk at charter time is the opposite error:
criteria written against a fresh incident encode the incident rather than the exposure,
and this story's whole correction is that the incident was the narrower thing. So
SPEC-0137 is written against availability, which is what actually failed, and the rest
waits for the work to be scheduled.

## What ratifying moves, and what it deliberately does not

- **DEC-0012** `proposed → ratified`; the reconcile activates it.
- **STORY-0019** is minted `proposed` and **stays** `proposed`. It is not in the effects
  list, because chartering is not scheduling.
- **No atom moves for Act 1.** The withdrawn instruction is an ingest-tree artifact on
  the reception plane, not a corpus atom; its lifecycle move is recorded there, and this
  document is the corpus-side record of why. That asymmetry is the honest limit
  `INSTRUCTION_LAYER.md` already states: `atomic_ingest/` lives outside this repository
  and no CI gate reaches it.
- **`unbound_claims` does not move.** SPEC-0137 is minted `proposed`, and the meter counts
  ratified claims only — so chartering costs nothing and scheduling will cost what it
  should, at the moment a rule and a control exist to bind it.
- **No pin changes.** The instruction that asked for one is withdrawn, and the ruling's
  entire content is that the pins stand.
