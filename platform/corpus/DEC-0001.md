## Rulings

**R1 — Credential carrier: the hybrid.** Transport identity is carried by
NATS-native credential chains; act-level authority is carried by caveated
tokens in the §9 grammar (ENT-091–097), transported in the message envelope
and evaluated by the chain verifier at gates and the bus auth callout.
Rationale: transport concerns transport; action authorization concerns
action authorization. Mixing the two forces the transport layer to make
claims it does not have the authority to make. Enacts ENT-097's working
direction as ruled; the token wire format is an implementation deliverable
of the chain verifier, constrained to implement §9 without loss.

**R2 — Version scheme: semver.** All atom versions follow semver
(MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH). Rationale: rule and control inheritance in complex
systems requires traceability and compatibility signaling between groups
and systems at higher scales; adopting semver from the start is a cheap
forward-looking decision, and remains cheap if the scale never
materializes, whereas migration from monotonic versioning later is not.
Enacted in ONT-010.

**R3 — Acceptance criteria: story-scoped specifications, not a type.** No
`AC-` prefix exists. An acceptance criterion is a `SPEC-` atom at story
scope carrying the `acceptance-criterion` tag and a `story_ref`. Rationale:
the structure is not distinct, so a field specification is valid where a
type is not; at-a-glance reference is an extensible-field concern (tags,
generated renderings), not a type-system concern. Tags carry no governance
semantics; gates key on scope and facet only. Enacted in ONT-045 and the
base-interface `tags` field (ONT-010).

**R4 — Multi-atom files: first-class under the atom encoding standard.**
File cardinality is not the governed boundary; the encoding standard is.
Atoms are delimited by paired, non-nesting boundary markers that are legal
comments in the host format — functionally concealable, so governed files
render as ordinary documents while remaining independently lintable per
block. Enacted as ONT-070/070a.

**R5 — References and inverses: the truth model.** Truth is temporal;
untimed statements decay to "true or false" at the moment of writing, while
time-specified statements are durably true-at-T. Mutation is a new instance
at a new timestamp; instances are immutable and the store is append-only in
instance space. References identify — by id, optionally pinned by version
and instantiation — and never locate; location references are fragile
couplings and are prohibited. A source of truth is an unbroken reference
chain, and two independent sources of truth for one fact are a source of
falsehood: every fact has one authoring chain, and all inverse relation
lookups are computed from the index, never materialized as stored reverse
links. Documents are assemblies of resolved atoms at a given time. Enacted
as ONT-014–017 and ONT-016's computed-inverse rule.

**R6 — Grammar sections ratified as drafted.** Document 0.5 §9 (limiter
grammar, ENT-091–097) and Document 0 §5.5 (exception grammar, ONT-055–058),
including the mirrored layer-separation restrictions (ENT-095 / ONT-057):
rules may have exceptions; credentials may only have limiters.

## Consequences armed by this decision

Ratification transitions both documents to `ratified` and unblocks, in
order: authorship of the per-type JSON Schemas (ONT-071) against R2/R3;
implementation of `atom-lint` (CTRL-0001) against the schemas and R4's
encoding standard; activation of RULE-0001 (the ontology enforcing itself);
and the enforcement-plane build. The chain-verifier deliverable is
constrained by R1.
