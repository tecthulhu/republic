## Two charter versions were withdrawn unenacted, and the pattern is worth the paragraph

v0.2 and v0.3 were each enrolled and each superseded while their ratifying pull
request stayed open. Neither was ever in force. The branch was rebuilt twice rather
than merged, because merging would have ratified a charter its author had already
replaced.

That window is a property of ratification-by-merge, not a fault in it: the floor's
signature is the merge, so everything before the merge is still draft, and a
correction arriving in that interval is the mechanism working. What it costs is that
an open ceremony is never settled — a worker who treats one as final will eventually
enact a superseded ruling. Both withdrawals are recorded in `withdrawn/` with their
reasons, which is why SPEC-0131 needed the state.

## The contract arrived first and could not be consumed

The principal-verifier contract shipped one delivery ahead of the charter version it
cites. Its digests were perfect, both halves present, pair integrity clean — and it
was unusable, because it hooks into `§5(3b)`, a step that did not exist in v0.3.
Reading the cited section rather than trusting the reference is what surfaced it.

**That is this charter's own argument, demonstrated on its own substrate.** Transport
succeeded completely and consumption was impossible. A receipt can attest that bytes
arrived; it cannot attest that a shipment was usable. Which is precisely why the
lifecycle terminates at `ACKNOWLEDGED` rather than at delivery, and why absence of
acknowledgement is a named nonterminal state instead of an absence.

## What v0.4 adds: the second identity, and its honest label

v0.3 gave the machine an identity — custody, evidence-grade, offline-verifiable. v0.4
adds the principal: a human approval per shipment, digest-in-push, txid recorded and
auditable against the provider's own log.

The pairing is doing real work. A machine signature proves *which machine shipped* and
nothing about whether a person meant to send it; a human approval proves *someone
approved* and nothing about where it left from. Neither substitutes for the other,
which is the same non-substitution STORY-0014 kept between floor-touch and human
evidence — the corpus arriving at one distinction from two directions.

**And the two are labelled at different strengths on purpose.** The machine signature
is evidence; the Duo approval is attested-with-independent-witness — a third party's
transaction record, not a cryptographic artifact. The register states each plainly and
never claims more than the factor provides. The first principal row is recorded as a
**self-attested genesis act**: every identity system bootstraps from a root no higher
authority certifies, and the honest move is to write that in the row rather than let
its existence imply a certification nobody performed.

## What is ratified, and what is not

Both documents are `truth_level: intent`. Ratifying makes the ferry's boundaries and
its verifier contract law *before* the ferry is built; P0/P1/P2 still have to be
chartered, built and evidenced like anything else. SPEC-0132 is not retired — that
posture ends when the courier is *live*, and a charter is a design.

SPEC-0135 is `check: human` rather than machine, because its central obligation is
that a provider's declaration of its own strength survives adversarial reading, and no
machine grades whether a sentence is honest. Dressing it as machine-checked would be
the overclaim the contract itself forbids.
